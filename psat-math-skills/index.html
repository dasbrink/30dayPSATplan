What Is on the PSAT Math Section? Every Skill, and Why It Is Familiar

By Dr. Brink, Ph.D., 32 years teaching mathematics

The short answer: the PSAT/NMSQT math section is 44 questions split into two 35-minute modules of 22 questions each, 70 minutes in total. A graphing calculator (Desmos) and a reference sheet of formulas are on screen the whole time. About a quarter of the questions have no answer choices, so you type the answer yourself. Math is scored on a 160 to 760 scale, and every question falls into one of four areas: Algebra, Advanced Math, Problem-Solving and Data Analysis, and Geometry and Trigonometry.

Here is the part most students do not realise: almost nothing on the PSAT is new. If you are in 9th or 10th grade, the skills the test is built on are the exact ones you are working through in class right now, and many of them go back to Grade 5, 6 and 7. This page lays out both halves, what the test looks like and how it is weighted, then the 280 foundational skills every PSAT math question is quietly built on. Get these solid in 9th and 10th grade and the PSAT, and the SAT a year later, mostly takes care of itself.

How the section is built

Two modules, 22 questions each. The first module is the same for everyone; the second module adapts, getting harder if you did well on the first and easier if you did not. The clock is 35 minutes per module and it does not pause between questions. The on-screen Desmos calculator is available for every question, and a formula reference sheet is one click away, so you never have to memorize the area of a circle or the Pythagorean theorem.

The four content areas, and how they are weighted

Most of the test is algebra in one form or another. Here is roughly how the 44 questions split.

  • Algebra about 13 to 15 questions

    Linear equations, inequalities, and systems. The single biggest area, and the heart of 9th grade algebra.

  • Advanced Math about 13 to 15 questions

    Quadratics, polynomials, exponents, and functions. Nearly as large as Algebra, and where 10th grade math is heading.

  • Problem-Solving and Data Analysis about 5 to 7 questions

    Ratios, percentages, rates, and reading data from tables and graphs.

  • Geometry and Trigonometry about 5 to 7 questions

    Area and volume, triangles, circles, and the first taste of trigonometry.

The skills underneath: the DNA of the PSAT Math section

Those four areas are the labels. This is what is really being tested. Below are the 280 foundational skills the whole PSAT math section is built from, the ones a question assumes you already have before it even begins. They are grouped the way math actually builds, from arithmetic up through algebra, functions, geometry and statistics, and the grade tag on each one shows how early it first shows up. For a 9th or 10th grader, this is not a list of things to learn someday. It is a checklist of what you are doing in class now, and what the PSAT will quietly test you on.
